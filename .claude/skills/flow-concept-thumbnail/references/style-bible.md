# Style bible: the faceless concept thumbnail

A breakdown of the 11 reference thumbnails the user picked, which are pastel Arabic explainer covers. Use it to make the concept, medium, palette and layout match that look. All measurements are at 1280×720.

## 1. What makes it work
- **One question, one picture.** Each thumbnail asks a question (كيف / هل / ليش) and the image is a single clever answer-shaped idea, not a scene with several things happening.
- **The idea is a twist on something ordinary:** a calendar becomes a tent for a sick man, chickens stand on pedestals that grow in size, a car is split open to show its battery. The viewer gets it in under a second.
- **Calm design, playful idea.** The layout is strict and identical every time (border, panel, title boxes), so all the personality lives in the metaphor and the panel color.

## 2. Metaphor patterns (from the references)
| Pattern | Reference | How to reuse it for a history/culture topic |
|---|---|---|
| **Exploded / cutaway view** reveals the hidden mechanism | an EV split into floating body, battery cylinder and chassis on a paper sheet, with a charging cable | a pyramid cut like a diorama; a ship's hull sliced to show its cargo; a castle split to show the siege tunnel |
| **Object becomes a place** | a giant desk calendar (month written in Arabic) turned into a tent, a man with a cold in bed under it | an Ottoman firman as a prison wall; a giant coin as a stage; a map folded into a tent for refugees |
| **Progression / comparison** | 3 chickens on 3 growing glass pedestals + a syringe on the floor | 3 crowns on growing pedestals; 3 rulers' hats; a shrinking territory on 3 plinths |
| **Tiny culprit under a spotlight** | a mosquito on a hand under a yellow spotlight beam | a small seal or signature under a spotlight on a treaty; a single bullet on a map |
| **Iconic gesture collage** | 2 men in 1940s suits and fedoras (B&W photo cut-outs) signing, between giant engraved hands making signs; flat peach and green circles; masking tape | historical figures as B&W cut-outs plus giant engraved hands holding a pen, a chess piece or a rope |
| **Isometric map / building with signals** | the Haram as an isometric model, with orange concentric sound waves from the speakers | Cairo in 1919 as an isometric model with orange ripples from the protest points; a battlefield model with arrows of light |
| **Extreme crop with a twist** | a bald head cropped at the eyes looking up at one curly hair sprouting on top, orange glow behind | a pharaoh statue's broken nose with a chisel; a mummy's eye looking at a modern object |
| **Quiet character + one telling detail** | a flat-illustrated oud player in a ghutra, with giant faint music notes and staff lines behind | a flat-illustrated scribe with a faint giant hieroglyph behind; a muezzin with faint sound arcs |
| **Landscape as a specimen** | a volcano island floating like a cut-out on a blue sea, smoke plume, sun disc, orange outline along the coast | the Nile valley as a floating cut-out strip; Andalusia as an island drifting away |
| **Symbolic still-life** | a decaf tin + a triangular coffee cup shaped like a warning sign | an inkwell shaped like a bomb; an hourglass filled with sand from a specific desert |

Rules of thumb:
- Choose the twist that **asks** the episode's question, and avoid any that reveals the answer.
- Use at most 3 objects, and give one of them clear focus.
- Scale play, such as a giant hand with tiny people or a giant calendar with a small bed, is the easiest way to make it feel editorial.

## 3. Mediums (pick one per thumbnail, never mix more than two)
- **Soft 3D clay render**: matte, airbrushed gradients, rounded bevels, grain on top. (calendar tent, EV, Haram, coffee). Best for objects, buildings, mechanisms.
- **Vintage collage**: B&W halftone photo cut-outs of people in period clothes, antique copperplate-engraving objects or hands (cross-hatched), flat geometric circles, a strip of masking tape, a sheet of paper as a backdrop card. (sign language). Best for history with real people, politics, treaties.
- **Flat grainy illustration**: simple shapes, gouache or vector with grain, gentle gradients, slightly muted. (oud player, mosquito, volcano, body silhouettes). Best for scenes, characters without real faces, landscapes.
- **Studio still-life photo**: real objects or animals on a seamless colored backdrop, soft side light, grain, slightly desaturated. (chickens, bald head). Best for food, objects, body topics.

## 4. Palette
**Fixed colors**
- Paper border: cream `#EDE7DB`, grainy.
- Title highlighter box: chartreuse `#E2E43E`. It appears only in the title, never in the art.
- Title text and logo: near-black `#111111`.
- Warm accent: coral/orange `#E8643A`–`#F08A5D`, as a blurred radial glow behind the hero, a sun disc, a peach circle `#F6B394`, an outline, sound waves, or a spotlight.

**Panel colors seen (choose by mood)**
| Panel | Approx. | Mood / use |
|---|---|---|
| Sage mint | `#AACBAE` | calm, curious, culture, language |
| Periwinkle blue | `#87AAE2` | science, body, cold, clean |
| Peach → coral gradient, pale blue corner | `#F2BD9F` → `#E8C4B1` | everyday life, warmth, seasons |
| Sunset pink → lavender, blue sea | `#DF8988` → `#979FC7` | nature, geography, epic |
| Burnt orange top + slate blue | `#C17245` / `#7A93B4` | night, tension, small danger |
| Warm grey-violet | `#595451` → `#918495` | music, nostalgia, the past, melancholy |
| Deep sage → olive / orange glow | `#727F6F` → `#52564F` | sacred, monumental, serious |
| Taupe grey + coral diagonal | `#7D7872` → `#65615E` | controversy, health scares, myth vs fact |
| Blue with an orange radial glow | `#5079CC` + coral | debate, warning, "is it true?" |
| Teal-green + orange glow | `#75AC8F` + coral | humor, body, light topics |

For **history**, lean on sage, grey-violet, deep olive, sunset pink or sand-beige with an orange glow. Keep the subject slightly desaturated so the panel color and the chartreuse title carry the color.

- Hero: centered around x ≈ 25–35%, occupies x ≈ 5–60%. It often bleeds off the bottom edge or sits on a flat plane (a paper sheet, a charcoal floor, an isometric platform) with a short flat shadow. It may sit slightly under the left edge of the title boxes.
- Logo slot (top-right): x ≈ 905–1215, y ≈ 58–232.
- Badge slot (top-left): x ≈ 62–170, y ≈ 60–172.
- Title block: right edge at x ≈ 1211 (≈95%). Two lines, each on its **own** chartreuse box that hugs that line's width (they are stepped, not one rectangle). The boxes touch with no gap, each ≈ 99 px tall (≈14% of height). The block's vertical center is at y ≈ 383 (≈53%), and the line-1 box starts around y ≈ 284.

- Grain: fine, even, monochrome, heavy enough to see on the cream border and on the title boxes.

## 6. Title copy
- Always a question, in 2 lines, with 2–3 words per line.
- Openers that fit Egyptian Arabic: «إزاي…؟»، «ليه…؟»، «هل…؟»، «مين…؟»، «إيه اللي…؟». The first line usually ends on the verb, the second on the subject: «إزاي اتبنى / الهرم الأكبر؟».
- The title says what the picture doesn't: the picture shows the twist, the title names the topic.
- Avoid exaggeration the episode can't back up.
